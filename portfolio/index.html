All work DE Medic · Healthcare · Germany

Making complex duty swaps simple for doctors.

A journey that lets doctors offer a duty, bid on someone else's, pick the best swap and get it approved, with every edge case and overlap handled along the way.

Role
UI/UX Designer
Company
DigiMantra
Platform
Mobile app
Feature
Duty Swap
Problem
Doctors needed to swap duties with each other, without overlaps, in an app used mostly by senior doctors.
My role
UI/UX Designer at DigiMantra. I designed the journey, every duty-card state and the edge cases.
Team
Add: PM, developers, client team.
Outcome
A four-phase swap journey where every duty's status is visible from the Duty tab.
Whois it for?

Doctors in Germany who need calm, obvious tools.

DE Medic helps doctors with their day-to-day operational tasks. Many of its users are in a senior age group and less familiar with modern UI trends and dynamic components. Every swap also passes through an admin, who has the final say.

Primary

Doctor offering a duty

Needs to give away or trade a shift and see its status at a glance.

Primary

Doctor taking or bidding

Wants to pick up a duty or offer one or more of their own in exchange.

Secondary

Admin

Approves the final swap before the schedule updates.

My role

I designed the Duty Swap feature end to end at DigiMantra: the journey, every duty-card state, and the edge cases between them.

Whatneeded to change?

A way for doctors to switch duties, without overlaps or confusion.

The core brief: build a feature and a journey that lets doctors swap their duties, while resolving every possible edge case and overlap. A swap is a negotiation between at least two doctors and an admin, so the design had to make each step's status impossible to miss.

Whydoes it matter?

Small mistakes in a schedule land on patients.

A swap touches two schedules, credits and an approval. If a doctor can't tell whether a duty is offered, bid on or waiting for approval, they double-book or miss a shift. For users less at home with modern interfaces, every hidden state is a support call.

Constraint

Senior users

  • Big, clear buttons
  • Less scrolling
  • Generous spacing between tappable cards and elements
Constraint

Many states, one card

  • A distinct colour for every duty state
  • Icons and labels as well as colour, never colour alone
  • Built for German doctors
Howdid we get there?

Four phases, two doctors, one clear status at every step.

  1. PHASE 01Doctor 1

    Offer a duty

    1. Opens Swap Duty and sees all their duties for the next two weeks.
    2. Taps Offer next to a duty.
    3. Chooses to keep the duty's credits or give them to whoever takes or swaps it.
    4. Offers it publicly to everyone, or privately to a specific doctor.
    5. The offer goes live in the Marketplace. In their Duty tab the card turns orange with an Offered tag: a globe for public, or a lock and the doctor's name for private.
  2. PHASE 02Doctor 2

    Take it, or bid a swap

    1. Sees a notification at the top and taps it.
    2. Lands in the Marketplace with Doctor 1's private offer pinned at the top.
    3. Opens it and either takes the duty or swaps it for one of their own.
    4. Can bid several of their duties. Doctor 1 decides which one to accept.
    5. On Doctor 2's Duty tab, the duties they bid turn purple, tagged as bids on a swap offer.
  3. PHASE 03Doctor 1

    Pick a bid

    1. Opens the app and sees the bids right on the offered duty card in their Duty tab.
    2. Taps the bids to view the duties on offer.
    3. Selects one and confirms.
  4. PHASE 04Both doctorsAdmin

    Swap and approval

    1. Once Doctor 1 approves, the two duties swap places on both doctors' screens.
    2. Each swapped card carries a note: under admin approval, updates shortly.
    3. After approval, the duty is updated in each doctor's duty list.

One card, every state

Each state gets its own colour, plus an icon and a label, so nobody has to remember what orange means.

Mon 14 Oct · 20:00–08:00Night dutyStation 3Scheduled

Default. A plain card with an Offer button.

Wed 16 Oct · 08:00–16:00Day dutyEmergencyOffered to all

Offered publicly. Orange with a globe.

Fri 18 Oct · 16:00–00:00Late dutyStation 1Offered to Dr. Becker

Offered privately. Orange with a lock and the doctor's name (example).

2 bidsWed 16 Oct · 08:00–16:00Day dutyEmergencyReview bids

Bids received. The count sits on the card itself.

Tue 22 Oct · 20:00–08:00Night dutyStation 2Your bid on a swap

Bid placed. Purple, so it never reads as an offer.

Tue 22 Oct · 20:00–08:00Night dutyStation 2Under admin approval

Swapped, waiting for the admin. Updates shortly.

Edge case

A duty can be offered and bid at the same time

A doctor can put a duty up as an offer and also bid that same duty on someone else's swap offer. The card shows both states, and once one swap goes through, the other is resolved so the duty is never promised twice.

All work EarthLink · Telecom · United States

Simplifying fiber checkout for a faster purchase.

I cut the fiber checkout from six steps to four, so an ideal purchase now takes three to four clicks.

Role
UI/UX Designer
Company
DigiMantra
Platform
Web + responsive
Flow
B2C checkout
Problem
Buying fiber online took six steps, lots of typing and many decisions.
My role
UI/UX Designer at DigiMantra. I redesigned the B2C checkout flow.
Team
Add: PM, developers, EarthLink stakeholders.
Outcome
Four steps, and three to four clicks for an ideal purchase.
Whois it for?

People buying home fiber internet online.

EarthLink is a US telecommunications company that provides internet services. It runs platforms for customers and for agents. This project is the B2C website checkout, used on desktop and on mobile through the responsive site.

Customers

B2C website

Choose a fiber plan and check out. The flow in this case study.

Agents

AC portal

Where agents convert leads and set up follow-ups.

Customers

CFS app

Track plans, payments and support after purchase.

My role

I design most of EarthLink's new features and concepts in Figma, and suggest product decisions that improve productivity and reduce customer support enquiries.

Whatneeded to change?

A shorter journey from plan to paid.

The goal was to shorten the journey for buying a fiber product, so customers check out faster.

Before6 steps
  1. Select plan
  2. Installation date
  3. Customer details
  4. Payment options
  5. Read terms
  6. Pay & checkout
After4 steps
  1. Select plan
  2. Installation date
  3. Customer details
  4. Review & Pay
Whydoes it matter?

Every extra step, field and decision is a chance to drop off.

Typing details by hand, picking a date from a closed component, and opening a cart on mobile all added clicks. On the responsive site the cart breakdown sat behind an extra tap, which broke the momentum of a quick checkout.

Howdid we get there?

Seven changes, each one removing a click or a decision.

Payment

Google Pay and Apple Pay

Added to the payment options for a one-tap payment.

Details

Autofill with Google or Apple login

Instead of typing, customers tap Google or Apple to fill their details. Two clicks in total.

Date

Open date cards

The installation date picker became open, tappable date cards. Fewer clicks to choose.

Date

First date pre-selected

After choosing a plan, the first available date is already selected. If it works, Save & Next is a single click.

Review

One Review & Pay step

Terms, payment and cart are combined in one place. No extra tap on mobile to see the cart.

Review

Due today, up front

The most important number in the bill, the amount due today, sits right above the payment methods.

Loading

Skeleton loaders

They replace the spinner, appear faster and help customers anticipate the next screen.
